# Neapolitan Shake

Like the dessert it's named for, June arrived with a little bit of everything in the carton — three distinct flavors stacked in the same tub, none of them quite bleeding into the others. So let's take them one scoop at a time.

![](/blog/june-2026-illustration.png)

## The first scoop: the Fed

The June FOMC was almost aggressively uneventful — no hike, no cut, no news, and, more pointedly, no responsibility. By "no responsibility" I mean that Chair Warsh made it plain that forward guidance is over under his administration, and that communication from the Fed will be deliberately limited going forward. Doesn't that remind you of Greenspan? Who's Greenspan? Yes — exactly. That was last century.

I'll say this without hedging: I disagree with this reform, fully. The old-school, let-the-market-find-its-own-efficiency philosophy of central banking simply does not map onto the era we're actually in. The market is an order of magnitude more complex than it was during the years when studied opacity passed for wisdom — more reflexive, more leveraged, more wired to every syllable. Cutting the cord and calling it discipline assumes the market is steered and ready for the new jungle. I'm not at all sure it is.

## The second scoop: the data

Both CPI and PCE came in fractionally cooler than expected — not dramatically, but enough to take some heat out of the room. The hike chorus, predictably, has started warming up again. I highly doubt it. If hikes were genuinely on the table, I don't believe they'd arrive before the midterms. Some songs only get sung after the votes are counted.

## The third scoop: the book

This is where June actually earned its keep.

$NOK gave us the run-up we'd been waiting for, and we took the profit — and then we stepped right back in. We think the name is primed for a revaluation after earnings on July 23rd. The math is uncomplicated: even before a single AI-RAN check clears the account, $NOK ought to be trading at least half of $CIEN's P/E. It currently isn't, and that gap is the trade.

$ORCL, by contrast, did not have a good month. In fact it had its worst month since the dot-com bust — a genuinely ugly tape. Why? Because we all hate dilution, and Oracle handed the market a $20 billion equity offering to chew on. But let's think this through instead of flinching at it. Why do they keep diluting? That's exactly the catch. You don't raise that kind of money to tread water; you raise it because the buildout in front of you is too large and too contracted to fund any other way. The dilution everyone is recoiling from is, read correctly, a tell about the size of the opportunity. We remain long $ORCL and we took our position into the weakness. We think this is close to a perfect entry for a long play — and one that should benefit from the rebalancing tailwind that increasingly favors the $IGV software complex.

## And dessert

I expect the Independence Day rally to show up roughly on schedule, the way it usually does, now that quarter-end and the bulk of rebalancing are behind us. The rest of July, though, reads like a genuine mystery. My base case is a bumpy uptrend through the back half of the year — the kind of grinding, two-steps-up-one-step-back climb we lived through in 2021. The direction is up. The ride is not smooth.

Buckle up.
