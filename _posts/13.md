# The Punt

May wasn’t great. The market wasn’t bad. And somewhere in the gap between those two sentences, I punted — which, if you’ve read these letters before, you’ll recognize as the kind of admission I’d rather make in print than have a reader infer from the tape.

![](/blog/may-2026-illustration.png)

## The punt: $PDD

The punt was $PDD. We’d anticipated a spike that didn’t arrive, and the autopsy comes down to two things. The first is structural and, in hindsight, obvious: $PDD trades on a U.S. exchange, but it is a Chinese company in every way that matters, and so it rhymes with the Hang Seng rather than the Nasdaq. The Hang Seng spent the month fighting through a low-liquidity zone — the kind of thin, airless tape where conviction doesn’t get rewarded so much as quietly ignored. The second is fundamental: regulatory fines left a mark on net margin, and the market noticed the dent before it noticed anything else. So the spike sat on the runway.

I want to be clear that I’m not walking away from it. Operations were good, as they reliably are. Growth is still there. Temu is doing perfectly fine for a business that the consensus keeps trying to write the obituary for. We stay optimistic, and we stay in.

## Staying long $NOK

On the other side of the ledger, $NOK continues to treat us well, and we are staying long. This one we love for the long arc — we’re positioning for a future built on AI-RAN, the convergence of artificial intelligence and the radio access network, and we’d rather be early and patient than late and right. Conviction, here, is the whole point.

The data, meanwhile, did very little to draw attention to itself. Everything printed roughly in line, the macro narrative held, and the only genuinely loud thing in the room was the rally in semiconductors — which has gone from impressive to frankly a little unhinged, depending on the hour you check.

## Looking to June

Which brings us to June, a month that looks, on paper, almost aggressively plain. All eyes are on the first FOMC meeting chaired by Kevin Warsh, newly installed at the head of the table. The question isn’t really about the dot plot. It’s whether the new chair knows which way the wind is blowing — and whether the “prosperity” that’s been promised can actually be delivered, or whether it stays the kind of word that sounds better in a speech than it reads in a print. We’ll find out soon enough.

One position we’re warming to as the page turns: software is finally getting some love from the hot money, and it seems the market has at last repositioned how it values CAPEX — no longer treating every dollar of spend as a confession, and starting to read it as a bet. We particularly like $ORCL in this trend, and we plan to take a position.

A plain month is not a quiet one. We’ll be paying attention.

— Atros W.M.
